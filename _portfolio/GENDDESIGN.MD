## Project Overview
The objective of this project is to use generative design to develop a lightweight skateboard truck hanger with an integrated motor mount. The design aims to minimize mass while maintaining structural integrity, with a safety factor of 2, and consolidating separate components into a single structure.

## Load Case Definition

| Name | Type | Magnitude | Location | Rationale |
|------|------|----------|----------|-----------|
| **Rider Weight** | Bearing Load | 1000 N (~225 lb) | Axle cylindrical faces<br><img src="/assets/img/rider_weight.png" alt="axle-load" style="width:300px;"/> | Represents rider weight under dynamic conditions. A value of 1000 N approximates a ~100 kg rider. |
| **Rider Weight ** | Bearing Load (Normal Force) | 800 N | Pivot cup interface<br><img src="/assets/img/pivot_weight.png" alt="pivot-load" style="width:300px;"/> | Represents normal force transmitted through the pivot cup due to rider weight and leaning during turning. |
| **Motor Torque** | Remote Moment | 6 N·m | Motor shaft center<br><img src="/assets/img/motor_torque.png" alt="motor-torque" style="width:300px;"/> | Represents torque generated by the Flipsky motor (6374 ). This drives the belt system and introduces torsional loading into the motor mount. |
| **Motor Weight** | Remote Force | 35 N (~8 lb) | Tegions around motor mounting slots<br><img src="/assets/img/motor_weight.png" alt="motor-weight" style="width:300px;"/> | Models gravitational load of the motor. A conservative value was used for robustness, as the motor is around ~ 2 lb. |
| **Belt Tension (Drive Force)** | Remote Force | 400 N | Tangential to motor pulley / shaft<br><img src="/assets/img/belt_force.png" alt="belt-force" style="width:300px;"/> | Represents belt tension during motor actuation. |
| **Belt Drive Torque (Axle)** | Remote Moment | 300 N·mm (0.3 N·m) | Axle interface<br><img src="/assets/img/belt_torque.png" alt="belt-torque" style="width:300px;"/> | Torsional load applied at the axle to represent belt-driven actuation. |
| **Turning – Case 1** | Force | 400 N | Lateral to axle (normal to cylindrical face)<br><img src="/assets/img/turning1.png" alt="turning-load-1" style="width:300px;"/> | Simulates lateral forces during carving. Represents shear due to rider lean. |
| **Turning  – Case 2** | Force | 400 N (opposite direction) | Same as above, reversed direction<br><img src="/assets/img/turning2.png" alt="turning-load-2" style="width:300px;"/> | Ensures performance under both left and right turning scenarios by reversing shear direction. |
| **Bump / Impact Load** | Force | 800 N | Normal to axle cylindrical face<br><img src="/assets/img/bump.png" alt="impact-load" style="width:300px;"/> | Represents transient forces from bumps or curbs. Applied to both axles. |

## Discussion
### Load Case Development
Rider weight wass applied as a bearing load at the axle, turning was modeled as lateral forces, and motor actuation was represented using torque and belt tension on both the motor shaft and axle based on the motor specifications. The kingpin region was treated as a fixed constraint These loads assume steady state equivalents of dynamic events such as carving and impacts. This approach captures the main failure modes including bending from rider weight, torsion from turning, and combined loading from the motor, but it simplifies changing conditions such as fluctuating belt tension and more transient motion. Improvements lie in better modeling of belt tension by separating tight and slack belt forces and accounting for changes during acceleration and braking. ChatGPT was used to help estimate reasonable force values and check their validity, but a more accurate model would incorporate experimental data from the pulley and belt system along with more realistic simulations, sucha as finite element anaylsis.
